Esta es toda la gramática de nivel A2 que necesitas. Se evalúa sobre todo en la Parte 5 de Reading (escribir una palabra en cada hueco) y en el Writing, pero también te ayuda a entender el Listening. Cada tema tiene explicación en español, ejemplos y un ejercicio con corrección.

1. Verbo be, have got y there is / there are

EstructuraAfirmativaNegativaPregunta
beI am / he is / they areI'm not / he isn't / they aren'tAre you tired?
have got (posesión)I've got a sister. She's got a dog.I haven't got / she hasn't gotHave you got a car?
there is / areThere is a bank. There are two parks.There isn't / there aren't anyIs there a bus? Are there any shops?
❌ I have 12 years → ✅ I am 12 (years old). ❌ I have cold → ✅ I am cold. ❌ Have you a pen? → ✅ Have you got a pen? / Do you have a pen?
En pasado: there was / there were.

2. Present simple

Para hábitos, rutinas y cosas que son siempre verdad: I get up at seven. Water boils at 100 degrees.

  • Con he / she / it se añade -s: she works, he plays. Terminados en -ch, -sh, -s, -x, -o: -es (watches, goes, does). Consonante + y: -ies (study → studies).
  • Negativa: don't / doesn't + verbo sin -s: He doesn't like fish (❌ doesn't likes).
  • Pregunta: Do / Does + sujeto + verbo: Does she live here?
  • Adverbios de frecuencia antes del verbo y después de be: I usually walk. She is never late. (always, usually, often, sometimes, never). Expresiones al final: once a week, every day, twice a month.

3. Present continuous

am / is / are + verbo-ing. Para lo que pasa ahora mismo o estos días: I'm watching TV. She's studying for an exam this month.

  • Ortografía: make → making, swim → swimming, run → running, lie → lying.
  • Palabras clave: now, at the moment, today, this week, Look!, Listen!
  • Verbos que no suelen ir en continuo: like, love, hate, want, need, know, understand, believe. ❌ I'm wanting → ✅ I want.

4. Past simple

Para acciones terminadas en el pasado, normalmente con un momento concreto: yesterday, last week, in 2020, two days ago.

  • Regulares: -ed (played, visited, stopped, studied).
  • Irregulares: hay que aprenderlos (lista en Vocabulario): go → went, see → saw, buy → bought, have → had, eat → ate, take → took.
  • Negativa y pregunta con did + infinitivo: I didn't go. Did you see it? (❌ didn't went).
  • be: was / were: It was cold. We were tired. Were you at home?

5. Past continuous

was / were + -ing. Para una acción en progreso en el pasado, muchas veces interrumpida por otra (en past simple): I was walking home when it started to rain. While she was cooking, the phone rang.

Muy útil en la historia de la Parte 7: It was a sunny day and the children were playing in the park…

6. Present perfect

have / has + participio. Conecta el pasado con el presente. Se usa:

  • Experiencias (sin decir cuándo): Have you ever been to London? I've never eaten sushi.
  • Acciones recientes con resultado ahora: I've just finished. She has already left. Have you done it yet? I haven't seen it yet.
  • for / since: I've lived here for three years / since 2022.
Si dices cuándo (yesterday, last year, in 2019), usa past simple: ❌ I have seen him yesterday → ✅ I saw him yesterday.
been / gone: She has gone to Paris (está allí) · She has been to Paris (fue y volvió).

7. El futuro

FormaUsoEjemplo
be going toPlanes e intenciones; algo que vemos que va a pasarI'm going to visit my aunt. Look at those clouds! It's going to rain.
willDecisiones en el momento, predicciones, promesas, ofertasI'll help you. I think it will be sunny tomorrow.
Present continuousPlanes con fecha y hora acordadasI'm meeting Tom at six.

8. Modales

ModalSignificadoEjemplo
can / can'tHabilidad, permiso, posibilidadI can swim. Can I open the window?
couldHabilidad en pasado; petición educadaI could read when I was four. Could you help me?
must / mustn'tObligación (norma, opinión fuerte) / prohibiciónYou must wear a seatbelt. You mustn't smoke here.
have to / don't have toObligación / no es necesarioI have to work on Saturdays. You don't have to pay: it's free.
should / shouldn'tConsejoYou should see a doctor.
may / mightPosibilidadIt might rain later.
Después de un modal va el infinitivo sin to: ❌ I can to swim, ❌ she cans → ✅ she can swim.
mustn't (prohibido) ≠ don't have to (no hace falta).

10. Artículos y cuantificadores

  • a / an: primera mención, profesiones: She's an engineer. I've got a dog.
  • the: algo concreto o ya mencionado: The dog is very friendly.
  • Sin artículo: cosas en general, comidas, deportes: I like music. We have lunch at two. He plays football. Y: go to school / to bed / home, at home, at work.
  • some (afirmativas, ofrecimientos) / any (negativas y preguntas): There's some milk. Is there any bread? We haven't got any eggs.
  • much + incontable / many + contable plural (negativas y preguntas): How much money? How many people? En afirmativa: a lot of.
  • a little (+ incontable) / a few (+ plural): a little sugar, a few friends.

13. Preguntas

Orden: (palabra interrogativa) + auxiliar + sujeto + verbo: Where do you live? What are you doing? When did she arrive?

What (qué), Which (cuál, entre opciones), Who (quién), Whose (de quién), Where, When, Why, How, How much / many, How often (con qué frecuencia), How long (cuánto tiempo), How far (a qué distancia), What time, What … like? (¿cómo es?).

15. Verbo + -ing o + to

  • + -ing: like, love, enjoy, hate, don't mind, finish, stop, go (go swimming); y después de preposición: good at drawing, interested in learning, thank you for coming.
  • + to: want, would like, need, decide, hope, learn, plan, forget, try: I want to learn Italian. I'd like to go.
  • Para expresar propósito: to + verbo: I went to the shop to buy milk (❌ for buy).

16. Primer condicional y relativos básicos

If + presente, will + verbo: algo posible en el futuro: If it rains, we'll stay at home. (❌ If it will rain). También when: I'll call you when I arrive.

Relativos: who (personas), which (cosas), that (personas o cosas), where (lugares): The man who lives next door is a doctor. This is the café where we met. The book which / that I'm reading is great.
